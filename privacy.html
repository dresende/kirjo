Kirjo Privacy Policy

Effective date: August 24, 2026

Kirjo does not collect, store, or transmit any personal data. Period.

  • No data collection — the app has no analytics, no tracking, no advertising, and no third-party SDKs. The privacy manifest bundled with the app declares no collected data types at all, and no tracking domains.
  • No accounts — there is nothing to sign up for and nothing to log in to.
  • Local storage only — which levels you have solved, your best pour count and time on each, your flawless badges, how many helpers you have left, and your sound and haptics preferences are saved in the app’s own storage on your device, purely so your progress survives between launches. Deleting the app deletes them.
  • iCloud — if you are signed in to iCloud, that same small record of your progress and your remaining helpers syncs through your iCloud account, so a level solved on your iPhone is solved on your iPad. It is held by Apple in your own private key-value storage. There is no server of ours anywhere in that path, and no way for us to read it.
  • Game Center is off until you turn it on — and the app does not contact it before then. Switch it on and your progress towards seven achievements — all of them counts of levels solved — is reported to Apple’s Game Center under your own player account, so it can appear in your profile. Apple handles that data under its own privacy policy. Switching it off stops all further reporting.
  • Purchases — the four helper packs are handled entirely by Apple’s App Store. Kirjo never sees your payment details, and the transaction tells us nothing about you. The identifier of a completed purchase is kept on your device for one reason only: so a purchase interrupted halfway is delivered once rather than twice.
  • Sound is synthesised, not recorded — every note you hear is generated on your device as you play. There are no audio files in the app, and it never uses the microphone.
  • Nothing else is touched — no location, no contacts, no photos, no camera, no notifications. Kirjo asks for no permissions at all.

Beyond Apple’s own iCloud, Game Center and App Store — all three acting for you rather than for us — Kirjo makes no network connections whatsoever. Because it collects nothing, there is nothing for us to share, sell, or lose.
